# ProcedureRoute

A procedure route draws on the map a published departure (SID) or arrival (STAR): from the runway to its last point, or from its first point to where the approach begins (Q56, 16/10). The eAIP's arrival tables end at the initial approach fix (LORNI, OKIPA…), 30 to 40 NM out: from there the controller guides the aircraft to the runway, and nothing beyond that point is drawn.

**Drawn on demand.** Pressing a place chip of a procedure ("ELCOB 7G") draws its whole route, in place of the ring the chip showed alone; it stays until it is closed or another one is asked for (a route takes longer than ten seconds to read). The sky's note says what it is and closes it: "ELCOB 7G · published departure from 26R", "LORNI 9E · published arrival to 26L or 26R", and its ✕ (`cl-sky__note--action`). The words that matter come first: when the note is cut, it is the runway that goes, and the help says it whole (measured 16/10 at 1 440 px: "… · departure from 26L · published route" lost 15 px).

**The line.** `color-map-procedure` (`#e0e0e0`, `color-map-light-procedure` on IGN and OSM), 1.6 px, solid; chevrons every 46 px in the direction flown (outward for a SID, toward the runway for a STAR); the runway's designator at a departure's start; an arrival ends on its last point's triangle, with no designator, since the line does not reach the runway. When the tables give the doublet ("RWY26L-26R") and the runways in use do not say which of the two, both first segments are drawn and the note says "departure from 26L or 26R". It is a reference, not a path: neither the aircraft's altitude colour nor the accent. Nothing ties the route to an aircraft (a chip belongs to a transmission, not to the aircraft followed): when the aircraft followed is the one that flies it, its trail shows as always (`color-map-trail-followed`), and the gap between the two is the controller's direct.

**The points.** Named reporting points and navaids as small hollow triangles (5 px), their name beside in mono 10 px `color-map-procedure`, on the side of the point away from the line: a name or a constraint is never crossed by a route; a point that several routes share is named once, in the widest gap between its lines (measured 16/10: one name drawn up to 21 times, once per route); the technical terminal points (PG271…) are not drawn, the line passes through them. Under the name, on the route asked for only (never in the layer), its constraints when the table gives them and they read without doubt (a doubtful one is left out, never guessed), in mono 9.5 px `color-text-tertiary`, in the brand's numbers: "≥ FL 060", "≤ 4 000 ft", "≤ 250 kt", "FL 070" when it is exact.

**The layer** (MapControls › Layers): "Procedures (SID, STAR)", off by default; when on, a `SegmentedControl` "Arrivals · Departures" (arrivals by default: the approach is what is heard), and the configuration it draws, said and not chosen: "LFPG west (26L, 27R), from the runways in use". It follows the runways in use already judged from the ADS-B tracks (the direction rule): no selector to keep in sync; while they are not known, nothing is drawn and the row says "Shown once the runway in use is known." The layer's routes take `color-map-procedure` at 38 %, 1.1 px, chevrons every 70 px, their named points at 60 %; the route asked for from a chip draws above them at full strength. Point names: below 67 NM across the sky, every named point; from 67 NM, only the points where a route ends (a departure's exit, an arrival's approach fix), the others as triangles alone. Measured 16/10 on LFPG and LFPO: the arrivals' points lie 30 to 40 NM out, so a cut at 67 NM like the labels' showed none at any width (0 at 34 NM, out of the sky; 8 at 67 NM, all hidden); with this rule, 4 arrival names at 67 NM and 5 at 102 NM, 10 and 14 departure names. Aircraft and their labels draw above the routes.

**A selected departure's possible exits** (Q57, the owner's addition, 16/10, in production 9fcc281). While a departure with a probable exit is selected, the published routes of the exits it could still take, from the runway it took off from: the likeliest as a route asked for (full strength, its constraints), the others as the layer's (38 %, no constraints); recomputed every two seconds, drawn again only when the list changes; the view does not move. Rules, as for the layer (review 16/10, read in the code, not measured: the test instance was down):
- the candidates are one set: each named point once, whatever the number of candidate routes through it (routes from one runway share their first points), at full strength if the likeliest passes it, at 60 % otherwise, in the widest gap between its lines;
- the layer, when on, does not name the candidates' points, as it does not name those of a route asked for;
- a route asked for from a chip wins: while it is shown, the candidates are not drawn, and they come back when it is closed (one strong line at a time; the same route is never drawn twice);
- said where the exit is said: the help of the story's key "To NURMO" ends "Its published route is drawn on the map, with those of the exits it could still take, while it is selected."

**Words.** The help of the chip, the note and the layer says what a route is: "The published route of ELCOB 7G, a departure from 26R. The controller often clears an aircraft direct to a later point: the route is what was published, not the path flown." An arrival's help adds where it ends: "It ends at OKIPA, where the approach begins: from there the controller guides the aircraft to the runway." The layer's row says it in general: "Published routes: the controller often clears an aircraft direct to a later point, so a route is not the path flown." A route is drawn whole or not at all: a point missing from the tables, or a designation heard that the cycle's tables do not have ("ATREX 7K" when they give 6G and 6K), and the chip shows its point alone, its help ending "ATREX 7K is not in this cycle's tables: its route is not drawn. Click to see ATREX on the map."; the variant is never guessed. Outside the French eAIP nothing is drawn: the chip of a procedure shows its point alone when it is known, the layer's row has no switch and says "Not for this station: published routes come from the French eAIP (aerodromes LF…).", as the source lines of Regional data.

**Data.** The coding tables of the eAIP (SID and STAR), per procedure: its points in order with their position, its runways, its kind, and per segment the altitude and speed constraints.
